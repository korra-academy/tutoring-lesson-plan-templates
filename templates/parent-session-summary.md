# Parent Session Summary — [Student], [Date]

Send within an hour of the session, while it's fresh. Aim for 5–8 lines — a parent reads it on their phone between other things. Copy the block below into a message or email and fill the blanks.

---

Hi ______,

**Today we worked on:** ______ (one topic, in words a parent recognises — "solving equations with brackets", not "Ch. 4.2")

**Went well:** ______ (one specific thing the student did, not a trait — "got 7/8 on the mixed questions unaided", not "worked hard")

**Still tricky:** ______ (one thing, framed as *next*, not *failure* — "we'll keep practising negative numbers next week")

**Before next session:** ______ (the homework, how long it should take, and when it's due)

**One thing you can do at home:** ______ (optional, small, no teaching needed — "ask them to explain what a factor is", "make sure the past paper is printed")

Next session: ______ at ______.

______ (your name)

---

## Rules for a good summary

- **Specific beats glowing.** "Great lesson!" tells a parent nothing and makes every future message sound the same. One evidenced win per session is worth more than five adjectives.
- **One "tricky", never a list.** A parent can act on one concern. Three concerns reads as a crisis and gets forwarded to the student as "your tutor says you're struggling".
- **Never surprise them in the progress report.** If something will appear as a focus area in [`progress-report.md`](progress-report.md), it should have shown up as a "still tricky" line first.
- **Same template, every session.** Parents learn where to look. Consistency is what makes a stack of these readable as a story in month three.
- **Copy the student in** (age 14+) or tell them what you're sending. A summary they haven't seen reads as a report *on* them rather than *for* them.
